## Added algebra and probability statements

Generic batch, denominator and callback facts now live in `ProductionAlgebra.lean`; `Production.lean` imports and reexports them while retaining the native indexed table consequences and actual AES specification import. This separates arithmetic proof dependencies without changing the security premises or claimed source semantics.

`VerifiableEncryption/Lookup/Algebra.lean` models actual quotient constraints `q_i d_i(x) = w_i` and a zero sum of signed quotients. Table multiplicities are arbitrary field elements; no theorem trusts a counting hint. It derives evaluation of the cleared residual as zero using multiplication, including at zero denominators. For `N` denominator factors of maximum degree `D`, the residual has degree at most `(N-1)D`. For grouped distinct scalar poles, evaluating at a pole proves the residual is nonzero whenever that pole's grouped residue is nonzero.

`Lookup/Challenge.lean` defines a finite normalized rational distribution and its event probability. If every challenge value has mass at most `δ`, any event contained in the roots of a *fixed nonzero* polynomial `P` has probability at most `deg(P)δ`. A uniform distribution is defined separately, giving `deg(P)/|F|`. The resulting lookup bounds are:

| Event | Bound | Additional algebraic requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acceptance of a fixed nonzero residual | `(N-1)D δ` | Residual nonzero |
| Any denominator pole | `ND δ` | Every denominator polynomial nonzero |

The acceptance bound already includes pole acceptances when the residual is nonzero; a separate pole budget is supplied for reductions that discard poles. These are unconditional finite-counting facts *given the stated fixed algebraic data and distribution*, not claims about Fiat–Shamir security.

The same module defines ordinary finite distribution pushforward, proving positivity and normalization by summing over fibers. For `r ≥ 1`, it derives the point-mass cap `rδ` for the callback map `root ↦ root^r` from the nonzero polynomial `X^r-c` of degree `r`. A uniform root therefore gives callback cap `r/|F|`. This proves the common-root power step; it does not assert callback uniformity or independence and does not establish the distribution of the production root commitment.

`Lookup/Production.lean` records a fixed pre-challenge batch of denominator polynomials, table multiplicities, and queries. Its query-denominator lemma derives nonzeroness from inverse constraints with numerator `-1`. Its indexed table includes both the native-field index and complete payload. True indexed membership yields canonical index bounds, the three existing AES semantic relations, and all twelve P-256 row payload columns with the caller's continuation. Scalar range membership yields `value.val < 2^width`, under `2^width ≤ Order`.

The concrete tagged-range denominator has degree at most `2^20`, and the twelve-column row denominator has degree at most `256^12`. Both polynomials have coefficient one at `X`, proving they are nonzero regardless of the other coefficients. Equality of row denominator polynomials binds the index and every payload column by extracting the constant coefficient and the twelve distinct powers `256^(j+1)`. Equality of tagged-range polynomials binds the field-valued tag and value; native natural tags are also bound when below `Order`. These are polynomial-identity statements, not claims that evaluation is injective at every challenge.

The specialized callback pole budgets are `N * 2^20 * rδ` and `N * 256^12 * rδ`, or the corresponding numerators divided by `|F|` for a uniform root. Distinct field-tag-or-value range evaluations agree with probability at most `2^20 * rδ`; distinct-index-or-payload row evaluations agree with probability at most `256^12 * rδ`. Both difference polynomials' nonzeroness is derived from coefficient binding. The stronger complete-residual result is developed separately below.

For scalar poles, `scalarResidue` groups arbitrary table multiplicities and the actual query occurrence count. An out-of-table query occurring between one and `Order-1` times gives a nonzero residual regardless of claimed table multiplicities. This explicitly addresses repeated queries and characteristic wraparound. The grouping of production gate traces into that model is not proved.

`Lookup/Kronecker.lean` proves radix digit-code injectivity and recovers multivariate coefficients after substituting variable `j` by `X^(B^j)`, provided total degree is below `B`. It derives preservation of nonzeroness rather than assuming that substitution is injective.

`Lookup/Multivariate.lean` associates each complete indexed tuple with a distinct affine linear pole. Specializing the root variable to one tuple's pole annihilates every residual summand except its own; the remaining product is nonzero by tuple/linear-form injectivity and the polynomial integral-domain property. The multivariate residual has total degree at most distinct support size minus one. Kronecker substitution therefore derives nonzero residuals for the actual radix-separated denominators when support size is below the source radix.

`Lookup/GroupedSoundness.lean` groups the *raw* table/query constraints by their complete tuples by summing quotients. This refinement uses multiplication only and remains valid at zero denominators. Repeated queries contribute their actual occurrence count; hinted table counts remain arbitrary committed field elements. An invalid tuple has no table contribution and has residue `-queryCount`, nonzero below the native characteristic. The source caps imply that characteristic bound. Thus nonzero invalid-query residuals are now derived for both actual custom argument shapes, including repeated tuples and arbitrary malicious multiplicities.

The new raw-batch false-acceptance bounds are `(n+m-1) * 2^20 * rδ` for tagged ranges and `(n+m-1) * 256^12 * rδ` for twelve-column rows, under the respective source caps `n+m < 2^20` and `n+m < 256`. Uniform-root variants divide the corresponding numerators by `|F|`. These bounds include pole acceptance; no unbudgeted conditioning on nonzero denominators is used. They require an actual invalid tuple in the fixed batch, not a premise asserting that invalid tuples already imply nonzero residuals. Their statement is about the source-aligned `Batch` quotient constraints, not the opaque extraction predicates.

`rowTupleBatch` instantiates the exact sixteen indexed ladder rows and twelve returned columns. A query outside the existing indexed table graph derives the invalid-tuple condition and the production-sized row acceptance bound. `sparse_range_bound` separately proves that the main-table checks on both `v` and `v*2^(11-width)` imply `v.val < 2^width`, using `2^22 < Order` to rule out native wraparound. These close deterministic row and sparse-range semantic steps, while their occurrence in the complete deferred/extracted trace still needs refinement.

## Actual AES coefficient graph and per-execution realization

`Lookup/MiMC.lean` models the installed BN254 MiMC source: 110 exponent-five rounds, zero initial state, field-addition Miyaguchi–Preneel updates, and exactly the two messages `[2, callback]`. Constants follow the installed `initConstants` algorithm: ASCII `seed`, an initial LegacyKeccak256 prehash, another hash for constant zero, then repeated digest hashing; each 32-byte digest is interpreted big-endian and reduced modulo the native field. `Hash256` is an explicit LegacyKeccak256 realization parameter. Its agreement with the actual hash implementation and byte encoding remains a source-refinement obligation; no arbitrary compression coefficient is substituted for the MiMC recurrence. An exact formal polynomial and evaluation lemma connect this recurrence to the coefficient used in the AES denominator.

`Lookup/AesGraph.lean` derives a nonzero *bivariate* residual for an invalid AES indexed query, including arbitrary hinted multiplicities and repeated queries below the native characteristic. Raw quotient acceptance forces that residual to vanish on the actual public graph `(callback, MiMC(2, callback))`. It does not derive a useful root bound on that graph: MiMC's formal univariate degree can exceed the native field size. `MiMCGraphBound` explicitly names a graph-root probability premise. It is neither an independent-alpha assumption nor a proved cryptographic property of MiMC.

`Lookup/Realization.lean` defines a gate certificate containing only table quotient equations, positive query-inverse equations, and equality of their sums. It derives `Accepted` from that certificate, correctly negating the query inverses. The staged strategy freezes its batch and oracle prefix using pre-challenge coins only; the post-stage receives the root and post-challenge coins. The canonical transcript places the commitment request between stages, recording the exact `logderivarg.Build` input order: optional variable table tuples, query tuples, counts. These committed field values are *not* treated as the challenge-hash preimage. The frozen preimage is explicitly the 64-byte Groth16 BN254 commitment encoding concatenated with canonical big-endian public/previous-commitment field encodings; the modeled configured digest takes at most its first 32 bytes and reduces into Fr, as in prover and verifier. Actual point encoding, frontend wire selection and configured digest realization remain backend obligations. The recorded answer is the root used by callback powers. On positive-mass samples, claimed root-hash and MiMC answers must match their explicit source functions, and the resource predicate counts commitment/grinding requests and MiMC evaluations across the transcript. A refinement of actual adversarial algorithms must record all relevant public local MiMC computations; the data log alone does not certify that accounting.

On each execution, explicit binding success relates opened values to the frozen snapshot, and explicit backend/trace realization supplies the pure gate certificate when the verifier accepts. Outside the actual graph-zero event, the theorem derives membership for every query of *that execution*. An independent compile-time table-equality premise connects that tuple membership to the precise indexed table graph; membership and a local continuation anchor recover the spread/substitution/chunk continuation. This avoids producing the impossible universal per-witness `LookupAssumptions` property from one execution. The existing extracted opaque predicates do not themselves imply these source certificates.

Oracle records allow arbitrary MiMC message lists, rather than only the coefficient's fixed two-message calls. Their answers must agree with the full modeled MiMC hash; the coefficient query remains exactly `[2, callback]`. This retains arbitrary public-function queries in the chosen adversarial transcript without assuming an inaccessible coefficient oracle.

## Ideal permutation graph algebra and explicit replacement boundary

`Lookup/IdealGraph.lean` now gives a concrete ideal graph bound for a frozen nonzero bivariate polynomial of total degree at most `D`, at most `q` cached permutation inputs, and a root point-mass cap `δ`:

`Pr[P(root, π(root)+root+offset)=0] ≤ (q+D)δ + D/(|F|-q)`, with `q < |F|`.

The proof budgets cached roots by `qδ`, identically-zero response slices by `Dδ` using a nonzero coefficient polynomial, and fresh nonzero slices by `D/(|F|-q)`. A normalized uniform-on-unused-outputs law and injective cached partial permutation supply the fresh response cap. A ring equivalence between the existing two-variable polynomials and polynomials with polynomial coefficients derives the necessary nonzeroness and coordinate degree bounds. This is finite lazy-sampled ideal-permutation algebra, not independent joint-uniform sampling.

The actual MiMC rounds and fixed-key permutation are now proved bijective using an explicit inverse exponent for fifth powers in the native field. The actual coefficient is proved to have the public affine form `π(root)+root+2*key`, where `key=compress(0,2)`. `FrozenMiMCGraphReplacementBound` separately names an **unproved** advantage comparing the same frozen low-degree graph-zero test in actual public MiMC and the ideal permutation response law. Adding that advantage gives a numerical actual-graph bound, conditional on the replacement premise. It is not ordinary secret-key PRF security, full public-code indistinguishability, or a claim that the advantage is small. A public implementation can be evaluated locally; any justified adversarial replacement bound must account for that operational access. Oracle resource accounting now sums MiMC message lengths, counting permutation-block evaluations rather than only hash API calls, and still requires a complete actual-algorithm log refinement.

For an invalid AES batch, `D=n+m-1` is derived from the grouped residual. If the callback cap is `rδ_root`, the conditional graph budget becomes `(q+D)rδ_root + D/(|F|-q) + ε_replace`, before the explicit binding/backend/source failure terms. The ideal bound and affine/permutation algebra are proved source; the actual public-MiMC replacement advantage remains a cryptographic obligation, not a theorem provided by these files.

`Lookup/DigestChallenge.lean` separately accounts for digest reduction bias. An ideal uniform 256-bit word is sampled from `Fin (2^256)` and cast into Fr, matching idealized big-endian `SetBytes` reduction. The proof establishes `2^256 < 6*Order`; each congruence fiber injects into `Fin 6` by quotient `word/Order`, since equal field remainders and equal quotients reconstruct the same word. Thus the ideal reduced digest has point-mass cap **`6/2^256`**, and callback power `r≥1` has cap **`6r/2^256`**. It is not asserted to be uniform on Fr. Applying that cap gives radix budgets `(N-1)D*6r/2^256` and an ideal graph budget `(q+D)*6r/2^256 + D/(Order-q)`, before the explicit replacement/binding/realization terms. Actual configured hash outputs, adaptive selection and grinding still require their separate cryptographic experiment assumptions.

The experiment theorem gives `Pr[bad AES lookup accepted] ≤ ε_binding + ε_realization + ε_graph`. The graph premise is stated for the same strategy, coin law, frozen snapshots and transcript budget, so adaptive query/grinding behavior is part of the chosen experiment. No random-oracle comparison or claim that graph coordinates are independent is made. A finite callback-union theorem gives the overall bound `ε_binding + ε_realization + Σ_callback ε_callback` without requiring callback independence. The staged conditional-law construction and finite-mixture theorem transfer the range/row budgets for each frozen snapshot to an experiment with fully adaptive post-challenge coins. Their conditional root point-mass caps are explicit distribution premises, not consequences of the staged data type. Conditioning on a selected/cached grinding query can invalidate a uniform cap; an actual reduction must bound each first query/candidate and include an attempt union budget. AES callbacks still require the unproved actual-graph premise.

## Production challenge boundary and source correspondence

* Custom `emfield/rangecheck.go` commits every main/tagged query and every hinted count. Main denominators are `challenge - value`; tagged denominators are `challenge - value - width * challenge^(2^20)`. Total rows plus queries must be below `2^20`. Sparse narrow ranges require both `v` and `v * 2^(11-width)` in the main table; dense ranges use a separate tagged table. This is distinct from gnark's standard range checker, used elsewhere by `bytes.go`.
* Custom `emcurve/lookup.go` commits all table columns, query indices, returned payloads and counts. Its denominator is `challenge - index - Σ_j column_j * challenge^(256^(j+1))`. There are twelve payload columns and the index. Total rows plus queries must be below 256. The model mirrors this one-variable polynomial, not independent random column coefficients.
* AES uses gnark indexed two-column tables `(index,value)`. The first coefficient is one; the second is `MiMC(2,callbackChallenge)`. The denominator is `callbackChallenge - index - coefficient * value`. Constructors populate tables using the same Go accessors used for the Lean tables. Source agreement and an existing test name are not a formal table-generation equivalence proof; no test was run here.
* gnark `multicommit/nativecommit.go` commits the union of inputs once and supplies callback number `r ≥ 1` with `rootCommitment^r`. Callback values are correlated and generally nonuniform. The model records that deterministic map, and `power_pushforward_mass_le` derives its point-mass cap from the root cap; `uniform_power_pushforward_mass_le` gives `r/|F|` for an ideal uniform root. Production commitment/hash values are not assumed uniform in any new theorem.
* Table `DivUnchecked` only enforces `quotient * denominator = count`. Zero denominator with zero count can leave a quotient free. Query `Inverse` enforces nonzero denominators. The models preserve this distinction instead of replacing unchecked division by Lean's total field division.

The concrete denominator degree and nonzeroness lemmas give `D=2^20` for tagged ranges and `D=256^12=2^96` for P-256 rows. The new invalid-batch theorems specialize these degree bounds and the source support caps. Their bound numerators are below `2^40*r` and `2^104*r`, respectively. This strengthens the algebraic argument beyond denominator nonzeroness and pairwise encoding collisions.

## Remaining obligations

The existing `LookupAssumptions` is retained. Its documentation now explicitly distinguishes its universal ideal semantics from probabilistic security, and describes the new modules without claiming they discharge the bundle.

1. Establish actual source/backend program interpretations and extraction anchors. Builder algebra now derives the raw gate certificate from the source-shaped frontend program, and local anchors compose its results into the extracted lookup conclusions. Producing the source-program proof and anchors from Go/backend execution remains an explicit trusted realization/extraction boundary. The current `Commit` and blueprint-related extractor methods are unimplemented, while opaques deliberately omit their bodies. Parameter generation needs actual LegacyKeccak256/byte realization. Source comparison and semantic normalization are not a checked Go-to-Lean/backend refinement proof.
2. Justify the actual MiMC graph-root/replacement advantage premise for the real adversarial strategy/resources, or change the production challenge design to one with an established algebraic reduction. Finite ideal-permutation algebra now yields the explicit graph budget, but its actual public-MiMC replacement advantage is unproved and not automatically small. The exact univariate degree bound is vacuous. Custom range/row radix nonzeroness and local dense/sparse/wide semantic composition are derived algebraically; the complete backend trace still needs its realization anchors.
3. Establish the binding/extraction and challenge-distribution reduction for the actual proof backend and transcript. The committed snapshot must be fixed before sampling; adaptive transcript attempts require an appropriate query/grinding budget. MiMC compression, commitment hash derivation and common-root callback powers cannot be silently modeled as independent uniform challenges. The generic point-mass hypothesis is a visible distribution obligation, not a proof of these cryptographic claims.
4. Instantiate the existing overall callback-union budget and per-execution membership/continuation results throughout the complete circuit trace. The universal idealized bundle remains separate; a probabilistic adversary/experiment theorem cannot produce an unconditional universal per-witness `LookupAssumptions` value. Full backend/adversary operational refinement must also substantiate the oracle/grinding accounting rather than trust an incomplete claimed log.
