# Parity evidence audit

An audit of the paper trail behind the 36 rows in `review-checklist.md` currently
marked `done` with a `PARITY` verdict. Read-only: no production code, test, or
fixture was touched, and `review-checklist.md` was not edited. Re-opening the
rows named below is a separate reconciliation step.

Scope discipline: this audit judges evidence, not code. Where a row's evidence is
thin it is marked unsupported and left there. No Rust was read to settle a row.
M01 is the single exception, because the differential oracle already settled it.

Method: the 118 queue rows and all 128 session-log entries were parsed
mechanically, then each `done` row was matched to the log entries that assign it
a verdict, and the git history of `review-checklist.md` was walked commit by
commit to find the commit that flipped each row to `done`.

## Counts

| Group | Rows |
|---|---|
| Supported | 1 |
| Unsupported | 34 |
| Contradicted | 1 |
| **Total marked `done` / `PARITY`** | **36** |

**Bottom line: 1 of the 36 rows claimed as done is supported by evidence that
meets the checklist's own bar.** The other 35 rest on a missing log entry, a log
entry with no evidence in it, or a recorder relaying gates it states it did not
run. That is 30 percent of the 118-row queue claimed as complete on a paper
trail that does not carry the claim.

The rows are also not the 31 the mutable baseline reports. The table holds 36
`done`, 70 `needs_fix`, 11 `needs_re_review`, and 1 `blocked_on_protocol_scope`,
against the baseline's `31 done / 59 needs_fix / 28 needs_re_review / 0 todo`.
The baseline's wallet paragraph states that all nine wallet rows are
`needs_re_review` awaiting an independent reviewer, while five of them are
already `done` and `PARITY`. The baseline block contradicts the table it
summarizes, which is the same desynchronization this audit is about.

## A correction to the premise

The task states that M01 was upgraded to `PARITY` with no corresponding log
entry. That is not what the file holds. M01 has two entries: the
`2026-07-25 05:21 UTC` review recording `DIVERGENT`, and a
`2026-07-25 12:25 UTC` entry that records the upgrade explicitly, including the
transition `M01 needs_fix/DIVERGENT -> done/PARITY`. It landed in the same commit
as the row edit (`ac364ba0`).

The substance of the concern holds, and the mechanism is worth stating precisely
because the fix depends on it. M01's upgrade entry is not a review. Its evidence
line ends "These checks were not rerun by the recorder", and the evidence it
offers is a restatement of the gates a fix worker reported in P06. So M01 was not
upgraded silently. It was upgraded on a relayed self-report, in the open, with a
disclaimer attached.

The genuinely unlogged upgrades are the five wallet rows, not M01. Correcting
this matters: 31 of the 36 rows have a log entry naming them, so a check testing
only for the presence of one would pass M01 and 30 others. Presence of an entry
is not the property worth enforcing.

## Supported

**A01** `sdk-libs/zolana-api/src/lib.rs`. Upgraded by `d21f1c25`, which carried
its own log entry (`2026-07-24 16:46 UTC`) in the same commit. The reviewer is
distinct from the implementer and names the implementation commit it evaluates
(`f5d698d9`). The evidence is a Rust-generated cross-language oracle rather than
a test tally: `api/transport-v1.json`, generated by
`xtask/src/ts_fixtures_api.rs`, pinned by a committed SHA-256, covering six
success exchanges plus HTTP, JSON-RPC, missing-result, and limit failures. Drift
against the fixture commit was checked and named. Commands were run and their
results recorded.

One caveat, not enough to move the row: the reviewer is identified by a model
name rather than a distinct agent identity, so independence rests on the entry's
own claim. The oracle evidence stands on its own regardless.

## Unsupported

Thirty-four rows. They fall into four defect classes, and the classes matter
because the remedy differs sharply between them.

### Class 1: no log entry for the upgrade (5 rows)

`W01`, `W02`, `W03`, `W05`, `W07`.

The session log contains only the original reviews (`15:05` to `16:24` on
2026-07-25), which recorded `PARTIAL` for W01, W03, and W05 and `DIVERGENT` for
W02 and W07. There is no entry anywhere in the log for the upgrade to `PARITY`.
The upgrade landed in `23a3ce76`, a commit whose subject is "record the G2-1 and
G2-2 signature acceptance rulings".

These five are the weakest paper trail and the strongest evidence of the 36. The
re-review was written into the table cells instead of the log, and those cells
are substantial: 1,700 to 3,000 characters each, citing specific files and line
ranges on both sides, and reasoning about reachability. W03's cell withdraws a
clause of the original finding as wrong. The same pass returned `BLOCKED` for
W06, W08, and W09 and `DIVERGENT` for W04, so it was discriminating rather than
rubber-stamping.

What is missing is provenance, not analysis. No timestamp, no worker identity, no
baseline HEAD, no commands, and no record of which agent concluded what. On
W07 in particular the original finding was that senders encrypt to the wrong
viewing key when the recipient has an active sync delegate, described in the
review as silent and fund-losing. A claim that severe closing with no attributable
reviewer is the case that most needs a name against it.

Practical note for whoever re-opens these: the analysis exists and is good. The
cheapest correct action is to move each cell's re-review narrative into a
properly attributed log entry, not to re-review the five rows from scratch.

### Class 2: logged upgrade with no recorded evidence (14 rows)

`I01`, `I02`, `I05`, `I06`, `I14`, `I18`, `I23`, `I30`, `I31`, `I32`, `I33`,
`I34`, `I35`, `I36`.

Upgraded by `f2f1a0e8`, which did carry its log entry
(`2026-07-25 11:40 UTC | interface post-fix re-review`). The entry describes
itself as an independent post-fix re-review, so attribution is present.

The entry records no evidence. Its bullets are `Baseline`, `Worker`, `PARITY`,
`BLOCKED`, `DIVERGENT`, `PARTIAL`, `Row transitions`, `Progress`,
`Exact next file`, and `Full SDK parity claim`. There is no `Explanation` line,
no `Evidence` line, and no commands. Fourteen rows moved from `needs_fix` to
`done` on a bare list of row identifiers under a `- PARITY:` bullet. Two of them
were `DIVERGENT` before the upgrade (`I02`, `I23`) and one was `MISSING`
(`I34`).

The row cells do not fill the gap. They are conclusory summaries of 91 to 177
characters in the shape "the named map, decoder, exports, and current-Rust
evidence now align", with no file, no symbol, and no command. Compare the wallet
cells above, which cite line ranges. Nothing here lets a later reader reconstruct
what was checked.

### Class 3: logged upgrade on relayed evidence the recorder did not run (14 rows)

`I03`, `I04`, `I08`, `I09`, `I11`, `I12`, `I15`, `I17`, `I20`, `I21`, `I24`,
`I25`, `I27`, and `M02`.

The first thirteen were upgraded by `506d8b9f`, which carried its entry
(`2026-07-25 12:07 UTC | interface residual re-review`). M02 was upgraded by
`ac364ba0` with the `12:25` entry it shares with M01.

Both entries disclose that they are relays. The `12:07` entry reads "Reported
gates passed: 29 Rust interface tests; 385 TypeScript tests with 1 skipped;
browser, API/export, dependency, package, and focused package checks. These
checks were not rerun by the recorder." The `12:25` entry carries the same
sentence over P06's gates. So the evidence is, first, a set of passing test
counts, and second, not verified by the party recording it.

Passing tests do not establish `PARITY`. The checklist says so itself, twice:
in the vocabulary section ("passing tests on their own do not establish
`PARITY`") and in step 6 of the review workflow. These fourteen rows are upgraded
on exactly the evidence the document rules out, and on a second-hand report of
it.

Three of them turned on relayed evidence in 27 minutes. `I08`, `I20`, and `I21`
are recorded `DIVERGENT` in the `11:40` entry, sharing what it calls the
encrypted-UTXO prefix validation conflict, and `PARITY` in the `12:07` entry.
Nothing between the two entries records a fix commit closing that conflict. This
is the same shape as M01 and is the closest thing in the audit to a second
instance of it.

### Class 4: recorder assigned `PARITY` at first touch (1 row)

`I16` `program-libs/interface/src/instruction/builders/create_associated_token_account.rs`.

Upgraded by `9f78e3a8`. Its entry (`2026-07-25 01:45 UTC`) states
"Worker: completed review report" and "No tests ran for this recorder update",
and records the transition `todo -> done`. The row held no adverse verdict at any
point and passed through neither `needs_fix` nor `needs_re_review`. A recorder
that ran nothing assigned `PARITY` on first contact, and the reviewing agent
whose report it relayed is not named.

## Contradicted

**M01** `sdk-libs/merkle-tree/src/indexed.rs`. Marked `done` / `PARITY`, fix
commit `4e271aac`.

`tools/wasm-oracle/report/w07-merkle.json`, generated
`2026-07-25T14:53:22Z`, probes
`IndexedMerkleTree::get_non_inclusion_proof` over 250 cases and records 233
agreements and a divergence sampled 17 times. On an empty tree of height 2,
queried at the sentinel value
`452312848583266388373324160190187140051835877600158453279131187530910662655`
(`0x00ff..ff`), Rust returns `ok` with a full proof while TypeScript returns
`err` with `INDEXED_MERKLE_TREE_INVALID_VALUE`, "Value is outside the indexed
range". The same probe records `get_non_inclusion_proof` error-code mismatches
sampled 75, 7, and 6 times.

`PARITY` asserts that behavior matches in both directions. One implementation
returning a proof where the other refuses to generate one is a direct
counterexample, so the verdict is contradicted rather than merely unevidenced.

Two honest qualifications. The report labels itself "Reconnaissance output. Not
a parity verdict and not wired into any gate", so it is positive evidence of a
behavioral difference rather than a completed review. And the task's further
claim, that Rust's own `verify_non_inclusion_proof` rejects the proof its
`get_non_inclusion_proof` emits, is not recorded in
`wasm-differential-oracle.md` or in the report; this audit takes it as given from
the task rather than confirming it, which would mean reading the Rust.

The oracle ran roughly two and a half hours after the upgrade commit, so this is
later evidence against a standing claim, not evidence the upgrading worker
ignored.

## The pattern

The upgrades are not spread across the work. Six commits account for all 36
rows, and three of them account for 32:

| Commit | Time | Rows set to `done` | Log entry in the same commit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `d21f1c25` | 07-24 17:46 | A01 | yes |
| `9f78e3a8` | 07-25 06:32 | I16 | yes, a recorder update |
| `f2f1a0e8` | 07-25 13:43 | 14 interface rows | yes, with no evidence in it |
| `506d8b9f` | 07-25 14:09 | 13 interface rows | yes, a relay |
| `ac364ba0` | 07-25 14:26 | M01, M02 | yes, a relay |
| `23a3ce76` | 07-25 17:14 | 5 wallet rows | no |

Three findings follow from this table.

**The clustering is real, and it tracks the batch entry.** Twenty-seven of 36
rows were upgraded in two commits 26 minutes apart, both driven by a batch
interface re-review. Every row upgraded through a batch entry is unsupported.
The single supported row is the single row upgraded through a dedicated per-row
entry. Batch entries are where the evidence disappears, because the format
degrades from `Explanation` / `Evidence` / `Verdict` per row to one line listing
row identifiers. Row kind matters only through this: 29 of the 36 are interface
rows, and the interface package is where batching was used.

**It is not a split between diligent workers and careless ones.** Every commit
to the checklist has the same git author, so authorship does not distinguish
workers, and attribution exists only in the `Worker:` field of a log entry. On
that field, only one adjudicating entry belongs to a worker that recorded
evidence and ran commands. The rest are recorders. The habit is systemic: 57 of
the 128 log entries carry the sentence "No tests ran for this recorder update",
and two more say the checks "were not rerun by the recorder". Nearly half the
log is relay traffic. The role, not the individual, is the failure.

**The one genuinely unlogged upgrade has a mechanical cause, and it is a rule the
checklist already mandates.** The commit that carried the wallet rows says so
itself, in its own body:

> Carried, not mine: review-checklist.md holds a concurrent worker's uncommitted
> row transitions for W01 through W08 (needs_re_review -> done/needs_fix, with
> PARITY, DIVERGENT, and BLOCKED verdicts). Their session-log entry is not in
> this commit.

The pathspec commit rule takes the whole current content of the paths it names.
The wallet worker's row edits were already in the file, so they were carried. Its
log entry had not been written yet, so it was not, and it never landed: the
wallet re-review's own commit `a6e2a383`, one minute later, changes exactly one
line, because everything else had already been swept up. `506d8b9f` shows the
benign version of the same mechanism, carrying the interface residual re-review
and its entry together.

This is the important part for choosing a fix. The table and the log
desynchronize structurally, not through negligence. Table edits live in the file
from the moment they are made and get carried by whoever commits next. Log
entries are written at the end of a session and are lost if another agent commits
first. The rule that protects against mixed commits is the same rule that lets a
verdict outrun its evidence. So the answer is not another instruction telling
workers to log their upgrades. The instruction already exists, as step 7 of the
fix workflow, marked "This step is not optional". It was followed here and the
entry still went missing.

## Recommendation on the enforcement check

Build the check. A rule is the wrong instrument, because the rule is present and
was not the thing that failed.

### What is feasible today

The table side is ready. All 118 rows parse cleanly from a fixed nine-column
pipe format, and Status and Verdict are single tokens from a closed vocabulary.
The check that would have caught the most, and needs no format change at all, is
the converse one:

> No row may be `done` / `PARITY` when the most recent session-log entry naming
> it records an adverse verdict.

That alone flags all five wallet rows and M01 today, from the log exactly as it
is written.

Two more cost nothing. The baseline's `Progress: N done` must equal the count of
`done` rows, which catches the 31-against-36 drift immediately. And every Status
must come from the documented vocabulary, which catches `T21`, currently sitting
at `blocked_on_protocol_scope`, a value the vocabulary section does not define.

### What blocks the full check

Requiring a matching log entry that names the row is not reliably checkable
against the log's present structure. Three obstacles, all in the log rather than
the table:

The heading's row field is free text. It appears as `A01`, as `M01/M02`, as
`C01, C02`, and as prose with no identifier at all: `interface post-fix
re-review`, `re-review | transaction queue T01-T31`, `evidence`, `fix`. A checker
reading headings alone cannot attribute the 27 rows upgraded by the two batch
entries.

Per-row verdicts inside batch entries live in prose bullets of inconsistent
shape. The `11:40` entry lists its rows under `- PARITY:`. The `12:07` entry's
`- Verdict:` line says "the 13 named rows are `PARITY`" and leaves the names in
the `- Evidence:` bullet above it. Resolving that indirection needs prose
parsing, which will be wrong eventually and will be trusted anyway.

Scanning bodies for identifiers instead does not work. Every entry names its
successor row in `- Exact next file`, so a naive body scan attributes a verdict
to the next row in the queue. This audit hit that and had to restrict matching
to verdict-bearing lines.

### The smallest change that makes it enforceable

Four constraints on new log entries. None requires rewriting the existing log,
and the check can run in warn-only mode over entries written before it lands.

1. The heading carries a machine-readable row list: `### <timestamp> UTC | rows:
   I01,I02,I05 | <title>`. Batch entries declare their rows in one place.
2. One verdict line per row named, in a fixed form: `- Verdict I01: PARITY`.
   This removes the "the 13 named rows" indirection.
3. `- Evidence:` must be present and non-empty for any row reaching `PARITY`.
4. A structured `- Verification: rerun | relayed` field replaces today's prose
   disclaimers. A `PARITY` verdict on a `relayed` entry is refused.

With those in place the full check is a short script:

> For every row with Status `done` and Verdict `PARITY`, there must exist at
> least one log entry that names the row in its `rows:` list, records
> `Verdict <ID>: PARITY`, carries a non-empty `Evidence:`, and is marked
> `Verification: rerun`.

Constraint 4 is the one that reaches M01, the 12:07 batch, and I16, which is 28
of the 34 unsupported rows plus the contradicted one. Constraints 1 and 2 are
what make the lookup mechanical rather than a prose search. Constraint 3 reaches
the 11:40 batch.

### On the commit-time hook, and a caveat worth heeding

A pre-commit hook diffing the staged checklist, refusing a commit that flips a
row to `done` without adding an entry naming it, targets the wallet failure
directly. It has a flaw that follows from the mechanism above: because a pathspec
commit carries a concurrent worker's row edits, the hook fires on the carrying
worker, who cannot write the absent worker's entry and whose only escape is to
bypass the hook, which the plan forbids, or to abandon its own unrelated commit.
That converts a bookkeeping gap into a deadlock between agents.

So make the commit-time hook advisory, printing what it carried and what entry is
owed, which is close to what `23a3ce76`'s author did by hand, and make the check
blocking in CI, where it runs against the whole file and no single agent is stuck.

### The structural fix worth considering separately

The desynchronization exists because the row verdict is stored twice, in the
table and in the log, and written at different moments. Generating the Status and
Verdict columns from the log entries, leaving the log as the single source of
truth, makes the class of defect in this audit impossible by construction rather
than detectable after the fact. It is the largest change of the ones here and
should not be bundled with the checks above, but the audit found no instance of
this defect class that survives it.
